Standard & Learning Objective

Arizona Standard:
Essential HS.P1U1.1: Develop and use models to explain the relationship of the structure of atoms to patterns and properties observed within the Periodic Table and describe how these models are revised with new evidence.

Learning Objective:
Use the periodic table as a model of sublevel filling to write full and noble gas electron configurations, identifying the energy level, sublevel, and number of electrons for each term.

About this tool

How it works

  • Your element is outlined on a blank table, and its total electrons are shown.
  • Build each term with its energy level, sublevel (s, p, d, f), and number of electrons. The boxes color in as you type. Stop exactly at your element, with no gaps and no red.
  • Noble gas problems: click the noble gas before your element first, then write the terms after it.
  • Support fades: on problems 1–3 the table colors in live and an electron counter is shown. On problems 4–5, the table colors in only when you click Check, and the counter is off.
  • After problem 5, you'll write a short explanation of how the table told you the answer.

Reading the labels

  • s- and p-block energy levels match the row number.
  • d-block energy levels are one less than the row number (row 4 → 3d).
  • f-block energy levels are two less than the row number (row 6 → 4f).
  • Rows 6 and 7: after the s-block, read the f-block row, then come back to the d-block.
  • Helium sits at the top right, but its two electrons are in 1s, so its box is s-block colored.

Table layout

Group 3 is shown as Sc, Y, Lu, Lr, and the f-block runs La–Yb and Ac–No. This follows the 2021 IUPAC provisional report on group 3 (Scerri, Chemistry International 43(1)). This layout lets the table be read like a book, one box = one electron. Many textbooks still place La and Ac in group 3.

Exceptions not used

These elements have measured configurations that don't follow the box-by-box pattern. They are never used as problems:

Cr, Cu, Nb, Mo, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, La, Ce, Gd, Pt, Au, Ac, Th, Pa, U, Np, Cm, Lr

Elements 104–118 aren't used either, because their configurations are predicted, not measured.
